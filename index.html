Sine, Cosine & Tangent

{{trigonometry|Trigonometry}} sounds enormous, but it starts with one simple question: if you know an {{angle}} in a {{right-triangle}}, what can you say about its sides?

Word detective tip: any word with a dotted underline, like {{hypotenuse}}, can be tapped or hovered to find out what it means. Words inside the bubbles work too!

1 Meet the right triangle

Every {{right-triangle}} has one perfect square corner. We pick one of the other corners to look from, and call its angle {{theta|θ}}. Then each side gets a name, depending on where it sits compared with θ:

  • The {{hypotenuse}}: the longest, slanted side, across from the square corner.
  • The {{opposite}}: straight across from θ. It never touches θ.
  • The {{adjacent}}: right next to θ, lying along the bottom.

Sine, cosine and tangent are three {{ratio|ratios}}: each one divides one side by another.

{{sine|Sine}}

How tall it is, compared with the slanted side.

{{cosine|Cosine}}

How wide it is, compared with the slanted side.

{{tangent|Tangent}}

How steep it is: {{steepness|up}} for every 1 step across.

Remember it with SOH CAH TOA

S=O/H   C=A/H   T=O/A

Say {{soh-cah-toa|SOH CAH TOA}} out loud a few times. It sounds silly, which is exactly why it sticks.

The big secret

Move only the size slider. The sides grow and shrink, but sine, cosine and tangent don’t change at all! Every triangle with the same angles is the same shape ({{similar|similar triangles}}), so the ratios depend only on the angle. That’s what makes them so useful: know the angle, and you know the shape.

2 Riding the circle

Picture a Ferris wheel with a {{radius}} of exactly 1. That’s called the {{unit-circle}}. As your seat goes round, the angle θ grows from 0° all the way to 360°. Your seat, the middle of the wheel, and the point straight below (or above) you always make a right triangle, and its hypotenuse is the radius: 1.

Dividing by 1 changes nothing, so on this wheel sine is simply your height above the middle, and cosine is how far you are to the side. Drop below the middle and sine goes {{negative|negative}}; swing to the left and cosine goes negative.

Why a wave?

As you ride round, your height goes up, down, up, down, smoothly, forever. Draw your height as you go and you get a {{wave}}. The cosine wave is exactly the same shape, just starting a quarter-turn earlier.

Why is it called "tangent"?

Tick "Show the tangent line". The green upright line just touches the wheel: it’s a {{tangent-line}}. Stretch your seat’s spoke until it hits that line, and the length of green is the tangent. Near 90° the spoke is almost parallel to the line, so tangent gets enormous!
